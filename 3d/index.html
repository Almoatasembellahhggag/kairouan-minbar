How to use

Drag to turn, scroll to zoom, right-drag to move. Click a panel to see its photographs and notes on the right, as on the panel pages; click beside the minbar or press Escape to close them. Double-click a panel to face it square on; double-click beside the minbar to go back.

Show turns the photograph, the drawings, the rail photographs and the stair photographs on the model on or off. View turns the model to one of its faces. Compare sets the photograph of before 1907 beside the model.

Numbers are those of the panel site: Saladin's, placed by the saved edits (N is a new panel, ? a place with no panel assigned), or Golvin's. They run over the side and the outside of the mihrab-side rail. The inside of that rail holds other panels: each is named after the one in front of it, with r. The maqsūra-side rail, which neither of them numbered, is q1 to q18 from the top, with r on its inside. Stairs: riser.panel. A panel recorded at one of these places shows its Saladin number there.

What this is built from

The mihrab-side face is Creswell's photograph (1940, Pl. 89a) laid flat and scaled to his length of 3.93 m; that gives a height of 3.35 m against his 3.31 m. Panel outlines are the ones traced on that photograph for the panel site, and the numbers are Saladin's (1899, fig. 52).

The stair has eleven steps, one over each of columns 3 to 13; their rise follows the slope of the stair string. Riser panels are counted from the fonds Poinssot photographs taken after 1910 (scans 013, 015, 018).

Stair photos puts the photographs taken after 1910 on the eleven risers and on the front of the seat (Bibliothèque de l'INHA, Collection Jacques Doucet, fonds Poinssot, Archives 106, 130, 03; reproduced for research and study): the front of the seat from scan 016, risers 11 and 10 from scan 017, riser 9 from 013, riser 8 from 013 and 015, risers 7 to 3 from 015, risers 2 and 1 from 018. Each board is straightened and stretched to the size of its step, and the frieze and the panels of the model are cut where they are on the photograph. Risers 1 and 2 come from a distant view of the whole minbar and are coarse. The first panel of riser 2 is hidden behind a post in that view and stays plain; so does the foot of riser 9, which the step below hides. The mihrab-side end of risers 3 and 4 is in the dark edge of the print. Shadows are those of the prints. With Stair photos off, the boards are plain.

The reconstruction drawings are the ones on the panel site, drawn from photographs and not measured. Each is fitted to the traced outline of its panel, so its proportions follow the panel on Creswell's photograph and can differ a little from the drawing itself. The drawing of panel 50 stands where that panel was before 1907, and that of panel 51 where the original panel was before its loss. The drawing of rail panel 66 is left out: that panel is not on the rail after 1907.

Compare puts Garrigues's photograph of before 1907 beside the model. Each pair of details is straightened to the same shape, so a panel can be checked against any place on the model. The stair rails count as places too, on all four faces: with Rail photos on, a rail panel is shown from the photographs of the new rails laid flat in 1907–10. A position on the outside of the mihrab-side rail is shown three times: before 1907, in those photographs of 1907–10 and in Creswell's photograph, which shows the rail by 1929. The middle one follows the panel: positions 6 and 13 hold each other's panels in 1907–10, so for these two it is taken from the other position. Where a panel is now is read from the edits saved on the panel site, in this browser if there are any. On the old photograph the outline of a panel is green where it is recorded in the same place, orange where it is recorded as moved and red where it is recorded as gone.

The place of a panel can be changed here: hold the panel, click its place on the model (it turns green) and press Now at the place clicked. A place is any panel of the side, of the two stair rails (the outside and the inside of a rail are separate places, with different panels), of the risers or of the seat front, the backrest, or the maqsūra-side face as a whole, so a panel that left the mihrab side can be followed. The change is saved in this browser, where the panel site's Edit page reads it too; Save file writes edits.json for the site's folder.

The model is lit and casts shadows, and the photographs take that light. They are black and white: each is tinted to the same wood colour so that they sit together, and its light and dark areas also give the carving a slight relief. Photograph, off, shows the plain model.

The steps follow the side: each tread lies at the foot of the triangle above it and the next riser stands at the triangle's upright edge, as scan 022 shows from inside the stair (fonds Poinssot, after 1910). Treads 2 to 11, risers 3 to 11 and the front of the seat are read from the triangles on Creswell's photograph, so the steps are as uneven as his photograph shows them (risers of 20 to 24 cm); the first step, which has no triangle, takes half the height of the second.

The carved frame of the rails, a vine-scroll band under the handrail, another along the stair string and narrow carved uprights between the panels, is photographed only on the outside of the mihrab side (Creswell) and, from inside, in scan 022. On the other three faces it carries Creswell's photograph: seen from behind inside the mihrab side; on the maqsūra side the bands run on and every upright is the one between panels 8 and 9 of the mihrab side. It goes with the Photograph button.

Estimated, not measured: the width (1.0 m), the height of the seat, the thickness of the sides and of the treads, and how far the panels sit back in their frames (8 mm). The posts at the foot of the rails and the spindles on top of the sides are measured on Creswell's photograph. The backrest of 1909–10, its columns and its urns follow the photographs taken after 1910 (fonds Poinssot, prints 012, 016 and 018) in outline only; their carving is not drawn. How high the frieze and the panels of a riser are is read from the photographs, which look up or down the stair, so it is approximate. The maqsūra-side face is left blank.

Rail photos puts a photograph on every rail panel of the three faces Creswell's photograph does not show, cut from the six photographs of the new rails laid flat during the restoration (Bibliothèque de l'INHA, Collection Jacques Doucet, fonds Poinssot, Archives 106, 130, 03, scans 073 to 075, 078, 080 and 082; reproduced for research and study). Each is straightened and set on its panel at the panel's own size: on the mihrab-side rail the panels are measured on Creswell's photograph, and on the maqsūra-side rail their widths and the uprights between them are measured on the restoration photographs. Black or white patches are breaks in the prints. The outside of the mihrab-side rail, the face towards the mihrab, keeps Creswell's photograph. Rail photos, mihrab face, off at first, puts the photographs of 1907–10 there as well; in them the panels of positions 6 and 13 are in each other's places. With it on, the numbers of those two positions, the place a panel is zoomed to and the panel a click selects follow the photographs of 1907–10; where a panel is recorded stays its place by 1929. Two prints of the rail in place taken after 1910 (scans 021 and 026) already show them where Creswell has them, and so does Creswell's print V&A 720-1929. With Rail photos off, the other three faces are plain panels.

The rail counts come from six photographs of the two new rails laid flat during the restoration (fonds Poinssot, scans 073 to 075, 078, 080 and 082), three for each face, joined into one image per face. Each rail was counted on both of its faces from a different set of photographs, and the two counts agree: 16 on the mihrab-side rail, which is also Creswell's count, and 18 on the maqsūra-side rail. The 18 are narrower, so both rails come out the same length. On the model the panels on the inside of the mihrab-side rail have the positions and sizes of those on its outside, and the 18 of the maqsūra-side rail are set on the same length of rail by their measured widths, good to about a fifth of a panel.
