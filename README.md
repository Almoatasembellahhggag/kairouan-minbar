# Kairouan Minbar Panels

An interactive comparison of the panels of the minbar of the Great Mosque of Kairouan, before and after the 1907–10 restoration. Panel numbers follow Henri Saladin (1899, fig. 52). Click a number to read what the sources say about that panel.

Notes marked "Identification" on the site are matches made by comparing photographs, not statements in the sources. Check the sources before citing.

Photographs: Saladin (1899), fig. 52 and Pl. XXI, XXVI, XXVII; Wikimedia Commons (détails du minbar; Un panneau sculpté du minbar); museum photographs and a catalogue page; Garrigues, Tunis, no. 271 (Wikimedia Commons); K. A. C. Creswell, *Early Muslim Architecture* II (1940), Pl. 89–90; Creswell prints in the Victoria and Albert Museum, 720-1929 to 726-1929; figures 11.2, 11.3 and 11.8–11.15 of Picotin and Déléry (2018), with the credits printed there: fonds Poinssot, Bibliothèque de l'INHA, Collection Jacques Doucet; Saladin; Strzygowski, *Altai-Iran* (Lehnert and Landrock); 62 prints and postcards of the minbar from before 1907 to 1939, Bibliothèque de l'INHA, Collection Jacques Doucet, fonds Poinssot, Archives 106, 130, 03 (`ph/ip*.jpg`); the stair sheet of the 3D model (`3d/stair/`) is cut from five of them; prints from the same file also supply the images shown for Saladin's Pl. ## Drawings

Panels with a reconstruction drawing show it first in their card, and `dr/` holds the full PNGs (`dr/web/` the lighter copies shown on the page). They are drawn from the photographs and from readings of them, not from measurements of the object, and two are marked unfinished.

## 3D model

`3d/` is a schematic model of the minbar after the 1907–10 restoration, linked from the main page. The mihrab-side face is Creswell's Pl. 89a scaled to his length of 3.93 m, with the panel outlines and numbers of this site; the reconstruction drawings sit on their panels. The stair has eleven risers with 38 panels, counted from photographs taken after 1910. The width, the height of the seat and the thickness of the parts are estimates; the posts at the foot of the rails and the spindles on top of the sides are measured on Creswell's photograph, and the backrest of 1909–10 with its columns and urns follows the photographs taken after 1910 in outline only, without its carving. Stair photos puts the photographs taken after 1910 on the eleven risers and on the front of the seat (`3d/stair/stair.jpg`, straightened from fonds Poinssot scans 013 and 015 to 018: the seat front from 016, risers 11 and 10 from 017, 9 from 013, 8 from 013 and 015, 7 to 3 from 015, 2 and 1 from 018). The frieze and the panels of each board are cut in the model where they are on its photograph (`STAIR` in `3d/data.js`), and a stair panel's card on the main page shows its photograph. Risers 1 and 2 come from a distant view and are coarse; the first panel of riser 2 is hidden behind a post there and stays plain, as does the foot of riser 9, which the step below hides. The three rail faces without a photograph in place are plain panels (16 on the inside of the mihrab-side rail, 18 on each face of the maqsūra-side rail, counted on photographs of the new rails laid flat during the restoration), and the maqsūra side is blank. The controls are three menus, Show (the photograph, the drawings, the rail photos, the stair photos), Numbers and View, and Compare; the instructions, the panel counts and the notes are folded below them. Compare puts Garrigues no. 271 beside the model: hold a panel of before 1907 (the model zooms to the place recorded for it) and point at places on the model to see both details side by side; the panels of the stair rails can be pointed at on all four faces, shown from the photographs of the new rails where those are switched on; where a panel is now is read from the saved edits. It can be changed there too: hold a panel on the old photograph, click its place on the model and press Now at the place clicked. A place is any panel of the side, of the two stair rails, of the risers or of the seat front, the backrest, or the maqsūra-side face as a whole; the change is saved in the browser under the same key as the Edit page, and Save file writes `edits.json`. Rail photos puts a photograph on every rail panel of the three faces Creswell's photograph does not show, cut from the photographs of the new rails laid flat during the restoration (`3d/rail/`, from fonds Poinssot scans 073-075, 078, 080 and 082). The outside of the mihrab-side rail, the face towards the mihrab, keeps Creswell's photograph; Rail photos, mihrab face, which is off at first, puts the restoration photographs there too, showing the state of 1907-10, in which the panels of positions 6 and 13 (Saladin nos. 56 and 62) are in each other's places; two prints of the rail in place taken after 1910 (scans 021 and 026) already show them where Creswell has them, as does Creswell's print V&A 720-1929. With that button on, the numbers of positions 6 and 13, the place a selected panel is zoomed to and the panel a click selects follow the photographs of 1907-10; where a panel is recorded (`slot` in `edits.json`) stays its place by 1929. In Compare a position of that face is shown three times: before 1907, in the photographs of 1907-10 (for positions 6 and 13 taken from the other position, so that the three show the same panel) and in Creswell's photograph. The rail panels of the model have the panels' own sizes: on the mihrab-side rail each box is the whole panel as measured on Creswell's photograph (larger than the outlines of the main page), and on the maqsūra-side rail the widths of the panels and of the uprights between them are measured on the restoration photographs. Numbers are Saladin's, placed by the saved edits as on the main page, or Golvin's; the inside of the mihrab-side rail, which holds other panels, is named after the panel in front of it with r, and the maqsūra-side rail, which neither numbered, is q1 to q18 with r on its inside; a panel recorded at one of these places shows its Saladin number there. The model is lit and casts shadows: the black-and-white photographs are tinted to one wood colour (each by its own average brightness) and their light and dark give the carving a slight relief, and the panels lie 8 mm behind the face of their frames, an estimate. Double-clicking a panel moves the camera in front of it, square on; double-clicking beside the minbar goes back. A click on a panel opens its card from the panel pages on the right, the same card with its photographs, drawings and notes (the model frames `../index.html?card`, which shows only the card); its Previous and Next move the model along, an enlarged photograph fills the window, and a click beside the minbar or Escape closes it. Compare takes its place on the right. Under each stair rail, on the inside, a triangle stands above every tread from the 2nd to the 11th (none on the 1st, where the post stands). On the mihrab side these are the backs of the ten triangles under the stair string on the outside, the same pieces: Picotin and Déléry (2018) take the triangles to have flanked the steps and risers, the one in the Louvre is carved on both faces, and those in the Bardo and at Raqqada are pierced. So each is the same place as its outside face and opens the same card. The maqsūra side is given ten at the same steps, numbered t1 to t10 from the top as its rail is. A pierced triangle shows the same openwork from inside, mirrored: scan 022, taken after 1910 from inside the stair, shows a triangle with the cross lattice of nos. 72 and 79 on Creswell's photograph. So the inner faces of the nine mihrab-side triangles that are pierced on Creswell's photograph carry that photograph seen from behind (off with the photograph). No. 76 shows its interlace on a light ground, not on openings, so it is probably solid and carved on both faces; its inner face stays plain, as do the maqsūra side's. The steps follow the side: each tread lies at the foot of the triangle above it and the next riser stands at the triangle's upright edge, as scan 022 shows, so treads 2 to 11, risers 3 to 11 and the front of the seat are read from the triangles on Creswell's photograph (risers of 20 to 24 cm, as uneven as the photograph shows them); the first step takes half the height of the second. The carved frame of the rails (a vine-scroll band under the handrail, another along the stair string, carved uprights between the panels) is photographed only on the outside of the mihrab side; on the other three faces it carries that photograph, seen from behind inside the mihrab side, and on the maqsūra side with every upright taken from the one between panels 8 and 9. The camera gets there in one smooth course: it starts and stops gently, pulls back on the way when the place is far off, and goes up and over the minbar when the place is on another face. With `?embed` the page shows only its Show and View menus and takes its edits, selection and numbering from the page that frames it; that is how the main page's Compare view uses it. `3d/lib/` holds three.js r128 (MIT licence).

## Other face

`back.html`, linked from the main page as Other face, shows the face of the minbar against the maqsūra on Strzygowski's two photographs of May 1907 (*Altai-Iran*, 1917, Abb. 167 and 168), cut from the researcher's 600 dpi scans of pp. 202-203 (`back/abb167.jpg`, `back/abb168.jpg`). Published on 18 November 2026 at the researcher's decision, although `sources/` is otherwise kept off the site: the book is freely available (archive.org) and Abb. 167 was already shown here through Picotin and Déléry's fig. 11.14; the photographs are Lehnert and Landrock's of 1907, whose rights were not checked. Columns are Strzygowski's, I to XII, counted from the high end, which on this face is on the right (p. 199 n. 3); III is missing ("III fehlt"). Panels are numbered in each column from the foot, which is this site's numbering: on Abb. 167 Strzygowski prints only 1 to 3 in the margin. XII 2 is the triangle. V 1 is cut off at the foot of Abb. 168 and is seen only on INHA scan 077; it is listed under Panel but has no outline. Columns I and II are very dark in the plate and their outlines are approximate (dashed, marked "?"). The design of each panel in its card is a reading of the photographs, except for XII 2, which quotes Strzygowski (p. 201).

Click a panel to see the photographs assigned to it. `back.html?edit` (Edit) assigns photographs: choose one and click the panels it shows, or tick photographs in a panel's card. Edits stay in the browser until Save file; commit the result here as `back-edits.json`, which the page loads for everyone. The photographs listed are the ones marked on the main page as showing the other side. The first `back-edits.json` holds the placements read on 18 November 2026: scan 083 (columns VI to IV), 061 and Picotin and Déléry fig. 11.14 (columns XI to VII, the figure with XII), 081 and 077.

## Editing

The main page shows the images, panels and notes. The Edit link opens `edit.html`, where you answer three questions for each panel: is the Saladin number correct, where is it after 1907 (same place, another place, or not on the minbar), and is the panel now at its original place new. After 1907 means the 3D model, which is Creswell's photograph extended to the rest of the minbar. Select its place in the 3D model opens the model beside the old photograph and takes the next click on it as the answer; the panel's own place counts as the same place. Another place can be a Creswell position or one that his photograph does not show: the inside of the mihrab-side stair rail (`b1r` to `b16r` in `edits.json`; the inside of a rail holds other panels than its outside), the stair rail on the maqsūra side (`q1` to `q18` outside, `q1r` to `q18r` inside), a stair riser (`r3.2` is riser 3, panel 2), the front of the seat (`s1`, `s2`), the backrest (`k`) or the maqsūra-side face (`o`). In `edits.json` the answer is stored as `cre`: `yes`, `moved` with a `slot`, or `no`. A `no` recorded before the 3D model existed meant only that the panel is not in Creswell's photograph. Compare on the main page and on the Edit page can set the old photograph beside Creswell's photograph, Saladin's figure or the 3D model: the model marks the place of the selected panel and zooms to it, and goes back to the view before when the selection is dropped, and a click on a place in the model selects the panel recorded there. A place with no panel of before 1907 recorded is selected as a panel of its own: the inside of a rail shares only its position with the panel on the outside (65 and 65r are two panels), and its card gives its photograph where the model has one, asks on the Edit page whether it is new or which Saladin number it is, and lists the photographs assigned to it. In Photos, Choose on the 3D model sets the model beside the old photograph: the panels marked for the current photograph are tinted there, a click on a panel adds it or takes it off, and a click on the maqsūra-side face marks the photograph as showing that side. Switch to Photos to assign each photograph to the panels it shows, including new panels marked N on Creswell's photograph, and to mark which balustrade it shows: the stair rail on the mihrab side or on the maqsūra side, each from outside or inside. A photograph of the other side of the minbar, the flank facing the maqsūra, which Saladin did not number, can be marked as showing that side. Edits stay in that browser until you press Save file and commit the result here as `edits.json`, which the site then loads for everyone. A browser that holds edits of its own keeps what was changed in it and takes everything else from the current `edits.json`, so an older copy in a browser does not hide what was published later and a saved file always contains it; the browser remembers the file it was last matched with, and `edits-history.json` (the earlier published versions) lets a copy from before 14 November 2026 find the one it started from.
