# Kairouan Minbar Panels

An interactive comparison of the panels of the minbar of the Great Mosque of Kairouan, before and after the 1907–10 restoration. Panel numbers follow Henri Saladin (1899, fig. 52). Click a number to read what the sources say about that panel.

Notes marked "Identification" on the site are matches made by comparing photographs, not statements in the sources. Check the sources before citing.

Photographs: Saladin (1899), fig. 52 and Pl. XXI, XXVI, XXVII; Wikimedia Commons (détails du minbar; Un panneau sculpté du minbar); museum photographs and a catalogue page; Garrigues, Tunis, no. 271 (Wikimedia Commons); K. A. C. Creswell, *Early Muslim Architecture* II (1940), Pl. 89–90; Creswell prints in the Victoria and Albert Museum, 720-1929 to 726-1929; figures 11.2, 11.3 and 11.8–11.15 of Picotin and Déléry (2018), with the credits printed there: fonds Poinssot, Bibliothèque de l'INHA, Collection Jacques Doucet; Saladin; Strzygowski, *Altai-Iran* (Lehnert and Landrock); social media posts by @HistoireTn and Mahdi Jouini (Facebook), and two Facebook posts of unknown source; an Alamy preview (PB954T). ## Drawings

Panels with a reconstruction drawing show it first in their card, and `dr/` holds the full PNGs (`dr/web/` the lighter copies shown on the page). They are drawn from the photographs and from readings of them, not from measurements of the object, and two are marked unfinished.

## 3D model

`3d/` is a schematic model of the minbar after the 1907–10 restoration, linked from the main page. The mihrab-side face is Creswell's Pl. 89a scaled to his length of 3.93 m, with the panel outlines and numbers of this site; the reconstruction drawings sit on their panels. The stair has eleven risers with 38 panels, counted from photographs taken after 1910. The width, the height of the seat and the thickness of the parts are estimates; the posts at the foot of the rails and the spindles on top of the sides are measured on Creswell's photograph, and the backrest of 1909–10 with its columns and urns follows the photographs taken after 1910 in outline only, without its carving. The three rail faces without a photograph in place are plain panels (16 on the inside of the mihrab-side rail, 18 on each face of the maqsūra-side rail, counted on photographs of the new rails laid flat during the restoration), and the maqsūra side is blank. "Compare with the old photo" puts Garrigues no. 271 beside the model: hold a panel of before 1907 and point at places on the model to see both details side by side; the panels of the stair rails can be pointed at on all four faces, shown from the photographs of the new rails when Rail photos is on; where a panel is now is read from the saved edits. It can be changed there too: hold a panel on the old photograph, click its place on the model and press Now at the place clicked. A place is any panel of the side, of the two stair rails, of the risers or of the seat front, the backrest, or the maqsūra-side face as a whole; the change is saved in the browser under the same key as the Edit page, and Save file writes `edits.json`. Rail photos puts a photograph on every rail panel of all four faces, cut from the photographs of the new rails laid flat during the restoration (`3d/rail/`, from fonds Poinssot scans 073-075, 078, 080 and 082); on the outside of the mihrab-side rail they show the state of 1907-10, which differs from Creswell's at position 13. The rail panels of the model have the panels' own sizes: on the mihrab-side rail each box is the whole panel as measured on Creswell's photograph (larger than the outlines of the main page), and on the maqsūra-side rail the widths of the panels and of the uprights between them are measured on the restoration photographs. Numbers are Saladin's, placed by the saved edits as on the main page, or Golvin's; the inside of the mihrab-side rail carries the number of its outside with r, and the maqsūra-side rail, which neither numbered, is q1 to q18 with r on its inside. The model is lit and casts shadows: the black-and-white photographs are tinted to one wood colour (each by its own average brightness) and their light and dark give the carving a slight relief, and the panels lie 8 mm behind the face of their frames, an estimate. Double-clicking a panel moves the camera in front of it, square on; double-clicking beside the minbar goes back. With `?embed` the page shows only its buttons and takes its edits, selection and numbering from the page that frames it; that is how the main page's Compare view uses it. `3d/lib/` holds three.js r128 (MIT licence).

## Editing

The main page shows the images, panels and notes. The Edit link opens `edit.html`, where you answer three questions for each panel: is the Saladin number correct, does it exist in Creswell (yes, no, or yes in a different position), and is the Creswell panel at that position new. A different position can also be a place that Creswell's photograph does not show: the stair rail on the maqsūra side (`q1` to `q18` in `edits.json`), a stair riser (`r3.2` is riser 3, panel 2), the front of the seat (`s1`, `s2`), the backrest (`k`) or the maqsūra-side face (`o`). Those places are easier to choose in the 3D model, which records them the same way. Compare on the main page and on the Edit page can set the old photograph beside Creswell's photograph, Saladin's figure or the 3D model: the model marks the place of the selected panel and turns to it when it is not on the mihrab side, a click on a place in the model selects the panel recorded there, and on the Edit page Pick on the 3D model takes the next click on the model as the panel's new place. Switch to Photos to assign each photograph to the panels it shows, including new panels marked N on Creswell's photograph. Edits stay in that browser until you press Save file and commit the result here as `edits.json`, which the site then loads for everyone.
